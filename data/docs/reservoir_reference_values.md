# Reservoir Reference Values and Reporting

## Passport Area

Passport area is a reference area value recorded for a reservoir in documentation or engineering records. It can be used as a comparison point for satellite-derived water area, but it is not a dynamic measurement for every observation date. A large difference from passport area may indicate seasonal change, extraction error, cloud contamination, ROI issues, or a real hydrological condition that needs expert review.

## Normal Level and Dead Level

Normal level and dead level are reference water levels from reservoir documentation. Normal level usually describes a planned or design operating level. Dead level describes a lower reference level related to limited useful storage or release. These levels are useful context, but the assistant must not infer exact water level from water area alone.

## Area-Level Relationship

An area-level relationship links water surface area to water level using validated reservoir geometry, survey data, curves, or reliable reference measurements. Without a validated area-level relationship, satellite-derived water area can support comparison and anomaly flagging, but it cannot safely produce exact water level.

## Anomaly Flagging

Anomaly flagging should identify observations that need analyst review. Examples include observed area much lower than passport area, observed area much higher than expected, high cloud percentage, conflicting NDWI, MNDWI, and SCL results, missing observations, and unknown method version. An anomaly flag is not an official safety decision.

## Monitoring Report

A monitoring report should summarize the reservoir, available observations, SCL water area, MNDWI area, NDWI area, cloud percentage, ROI area, comparison with passport area, detected issues, methodological notes, sources, and a short conclusion. The conclusion should include limitations and should not claim exact water level unless validated area-level reference data is available.
